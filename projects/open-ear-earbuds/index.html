Open-Ear Wired Earbuds: A 3D-Printed Build

I designed and 3D-printed wired open-ear earphones around a driver salvaged from a pair of wired earphones, plus a pocket case with a cable reel to stop cords tangling. The pod sits in front of the ear canal and an arm hooks over the ear. Nothing goes into the canal.

⚠️

The problem

Sealed earbuds block traffic and alarms, get sweaty and tiring, fail within weeks to months when cheap, and tangle in a pocket.

👂

The solution

A wired, passive open-ear design: no battery, pairing, audio delay or radio. Repairable by reprinting one part, and it reuses a driver that would otherwise be thrown away.

📐

Parametric design

One Blender script builds the shell and cap with exact booleans. Shell depth is derived from the driver (14.4 mm across, 7.4 mm thick), and one setting mirrors the arm for the other ear. Parts snap together with no glue.

🧵

Case with cable reel

An oblong clamshell (about 116 × 56 mm) that holds both earbuds and winds both cables onto a reel with one turn of a knob. Printed, with both earbuds seated in their grooves and the cables wound onto the orange reel. See the photos above.

Iterations

1 · Driver wouldn't fit

Widened the cavity from 13.6 to 15 mm.

2 · A 3 mm PLA arm snapped

Raised the rise to 14 mm, thickened to 3.5 mm, moved to PETG or TPU.

3 · Low volume from leakage

Added a mesh front wall and a clamp ring that presses the foam rim to seal front from back.

4–5 · Cap popping out, then sitting proud

Added a snap groove and ridge, then measured the can (6.6 mm) and made shell depth a formula of driver thickness.

6–7 · Wrong pointing, bulky look

Regenerated the right ear as a true mirror, then replaced the flat disc with an egg-shaped pod, with slim and compact variants.

Known issues

Glasses temple near the arm, rough support residue at the arm top, and the cable pulls the pod slightly forward.

Limits: open-ear sound is quieter with less bass than in-ear, so it suits quiet to moderate places. The fit is tuned to my ears. Next: measure the foam, print the left egg shell, test sound with a louder driver, and pick a final design.

FAQ

Questions

What are these earphones?

Wired, passive open-ear earphones: a pod sits in front of the ear canal and a printed arm hooks over the ear, so nothing enters the canal. They use a driver salvaged from a pair of wired earphones.

How are they designed?

In a single parametric Blender Python script that builds the shell and cap with exact booleans. Changing the driver size rebuilds the parts, and one setting mirrors the arm for the other ear.

What is tested and what is not?

Both ears were printed and worn and the open-ear fit works. Sound quality and volume and the slim and compact shells have not been tested yet.

What are the limits?

Open-ear sound is quieter with less bass than in-ear, so it suits quiet to moderate places. The fit is tuned to the maker's ears and the design is not tested for sweat or water.
